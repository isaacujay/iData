# IData Business Portfolio

**A multi-page, mobile-first business website for IData, a data, AI and software delivery company.**

> *IData turns business data into decisions through AI, analytics and software, delivered by a trusted team.*

This document is the single source of truth for anyone joining the project: developers, designers, content writers, QA and project managers. It explains what the site is, why it exists, how every page behaves, how the system is built, where every piece of data lives, how to run it, and how to ship it.

## 1. What IData is

**IData** is a business brand, not a personal portfolio. It sells four service areas:

| Service area | Example offerings |
|---|---|
| **AI and Agentic Systems** | Multi-agent task orchestration and tool calling, enterprise RAG and private document reasoning, evaluations and guardrails, AI analysts and automated reporting agents |
| **Data Analytics and Dashboards** | Executive dashboards, automated ETL pipelines, single-source-of-truth metrics modelling, sales, marketing and finance dashboards, KPI scorecards |
| **Data Science and Machine Learning** | Churn and lifetime value models, pricing and demand forecasting, lead scoring, segmentation, fraud and risk detection, production ML pipelines |
| **Software and Web Development** | Custom SaaS products, internal tools, payment and CRM integrations, React, Next.js and TypeScript front ends, serverless back ends |

### Delivery model

IData is delivered by **a core team plus vetted freelancers**, so work is not limited to one person's capacity. The site therefore presents a team, leadership and a delivery process, and includes a client portal so projects can be tracked in one place regardless of who does the work.

Delivery flow: **request, scoping, assignment to a team member or freelancer, delivery, internal review, handover.** Every delivery is reviewed by a core member before the client sees it.

### Brand tone

Corporate and trustworthy. Plain language, confident, no hype, no jargon.

## 3. Audiences and what they can do

**Audiences:** international clients (freelance and remote), larger companies and agencies, Nigerian SMEs and startups. Secondary: prospective team members and freelancers.

**A visitor can:**

- Explore the four services and their sub-offerings.
- Watch a demo video (at most three on the whole site).
- Browse case studies and filter them.
- Read client reviews and leave one (published only after approval).
- Meet the leadership, core team and extended network.
- Request a quote through a form (with an optional file).
- Book a 30-minute call through Calendly.
- Search the whole site (Ctrl+K or Cmd+K, or the search icon).
- Sign in to the client portal to follow projects, milestones, files and messages.

**IData staff can** use the admin area to manage leads, approve reviews, create projects, assign members and publish deliverables.

### 4.3 Navigation model (Observed and Specified)

The owner does **not** want a long list of header links. Navigation works like this:

- **Slim sticky header:** logo on the left; on the right a search icon, a **Menu** button and one high-contrast **Request a Quote** button. On desktop, at most three plain links (Services, Work, Reviews).
- **Full-screen menu** (`FullScreenMenu.tsx`). Observed layout, three columns:
  - *Services and Capabilities:* Services Overview, with the four services beneath it.
  - *Agency and Proof:* Work and Demos, Client Reviews, Leadership and Team, About and Delivery, Contact and Quotes.
  - *Client Area and Actions:* Client Portal, Admin Console (IData Team), the **Request a Quote** button and the **Book a 30-min Call** button.
  - Bottom bar: LinkedIn, GitHub, X / Twitter, location line (Lagos, Nigeria and Remote Worldwide) and the contact email. The menu also contains a search field with the Ctrl+K hint.
- **Command palette** (`CommandPalette.tsx`): site-wide search across services, case studies, team and FAQs.
- **CTA-first navigation:** every page ends with a **Next step** band of two or three cards pointing to related pages. Service pages link to matching case studies and reviews.
- **Mobile:** a slim bottom bar with Menu, Search and Quote.
- Breadcrumbs on inner pages (`Breadcrumbs.tsx`) and a back-to-top button.

**Hard rule: no numbering in the interface.** No `01`, `02`, `03` and no `1.`, `2.`, `3.` in menus, navigation links, cards or section labels. Menu items are plain, large words.

**Hard rule: proper headings.** One `h1` per page, `h2` for sections, `h3` for cards and sub-points, never skipping levels.

## 5. Page-by-page specification

### 5.1 Home (`/`)

Must be short, about three screens on desktop. Sections, in order, and no more:

1. **Hero:** animated gradient mesh background, short headline revealed word by word, one supporting line, two buttons (Request a Quote, Book a Call), a small row of trust signals, and a floating dashboard graphic that reacts to the mouse.
2. **Continuous marquee strip** (`Marquee.tsx`): an infinite horizontal ticker (logo marquee or logo slider) of tools and technologies, later client logos. Two rows moving in opposite directions, pause on hover, edge fade, seamless loop. Items come from `content/partners.ts`.
3. **Services:** four interactive cards (icon, one-line promise, three bullets, Explore Service CTA), cursor spotlight and slight 3D tilt.
4. **Featured demo:** one large click-to-load video beside three proof points with animated counters (`content/stats.ts`).
5. **Reviews strip:** auto-scrolling carousel of three visible review cards, with a **Read all reviews** CTA.
6. **Final call-to-action band:** Request a Quote and Book a Call.
7. **Footer.**

Do not repeat content that has its own page. Teaser plus CTA only.

### 5.2 Services overview and service detail

- Overview: four cards with a consistent template.
- Detail template: problem solved, what the client receives, process steps, demo video, related case study, related reviews, FAQ, and a Next step band.
- All four detail pages share **one template** driven by `content/services.ts`.

### 5.3 Work and demos

Filterable gallery. Each case study card shows cover or video thumbnail, client type tag, one result headline, tools row, and on hover a short summary with an Open CTA. Detail page: challenge, solution, results, tools, media, and a **Start a similar project** CTA.

### 5.4 Reviews (`/reviews`)

Observed: filter chips (All Reviews, Agentic AI, Analytics and BI, Data Science and ML, Software Engineering), a **Client Segment** dropdown (All Types and others), and a grid of review cards with a star rating, a **Verified Client** badge, quote text, avatar initial, name, role and company.

Specified in full:

1. Summary header: average rating, count, rating breakdown bar.
2. Featured carousel: auto-play, swipe, arrows and dots, pause on hover.
3. Two opposing marquee rows of one-line reviews.
4. Filter chips by service and by client type (international, agency, SME).
5. Masonry wall of all approved reviews with a Load more button.
6. **Leave a review** form: name, company, role, service, rating, text, optional photo. Submissions are stored as `pending` and appear only after an admin approves them.
7. Next step band.

> **Integrity warning.** The seeded reviews, partner names and statistics in `content/` are **placeholders written by the AI agent**. They must be replaced with real, permitted client content before launch, or clearly labelled as samples. The **Verified Client** badge must only appear on real reviews approved by an admin. Publishing invented testimonials as real would be misleading and could create legal and reputational risk.

### 5.5 Team (`/team`)

Three layers, all driven by `content/team.ts`:

1. **Leadership:** large featured cards for the CEO and other leaders (for example Founder and CEO, CTO, Head of Data and AI, Head of Delivery, Head of Business Development). Big photo, name, title, two-line bio, three focus areas, social links. Clicking opens a detail panel with the full bio and the services that person leads.
2. **Core team:** grid grouped by service area (AI, Data, Software, Operations) with filter chips.
3. **Extended network:** vetted freelancers and partners in smaller cards, with a note explaining how IData vets and reviews freelance work. Controlled by a config flag.

Person entry fields: `id`, `name`, `title`, `level` (leadership, core, extended), `area`, `photo`, `bio`, `focus` (list), `links` (LinkedIn, GitHub, email), `order`. Adding a person is one new entry plus one photo in `public/images/team/`.

### 5.6 About (`/about`)

Mission, values, delivery process (`content/process.ts`), FAQs (`content/faqs.ts`), and a **Meet the leadership** teaser with a CTA to the Team page.

### 5.7 Contact (`/contact`)

- **Quote form:** name, email, company, service of interest, budget range, project description, optional file (maximum 5 MB), consent checkbox.
- **Booking:** Calendly inline embed, plus a modal opened from Book a Call buttons (`CalendlyModal.tsx`).
- Success state shows Next step cards. Error state shows a mailto fallback.

### 5.8 Client portal (`/portal`)

Secure sign-up and sign-in (email and password, plus Google sign-in). Dashboard listing the client's projects with status and milestones, deliverable downloads, and a message thread per project. A client sees **only their own** projects.

### 5.9 Admin (`/admin`)

IData staff only. View and update leads (new, contacted, qualified, won, lost), approve or reject reviews, create projects, assign members, publish deliverables.

### 5.10 Legal and 404

Privacy policy and terms from `content/legal/`. The 404 page offers CTA cards back to useful pages.

### 5.11 Footer (Observed)

A professional footer on every page:

- Brand block: logo, one-sentence description, LinkedIn, GitHub and X (Twitter) links.
- **Services** column: the four service links.
- **Company** column: About and Process, Leadership and Team, Work and Demos, Client Reviews, Contact and Booking.
- **Client Area and Updates** column: Client Portal Sign-In, support email, Delivery FAQs, and an engineering newsletter signup with a **Join** button (saves to the database).
- Bottom bar: copyright, Privacy, Terms, back to top.
- Responsive: columns stack into expandable sections on mobile.

## 6. Design system

### 6.1 Theme history and current direction

| Stage | Direction |
|---|---|
| Original brief | Dark theme from four owner colors: `#000022`, `#001242`, `#040f16`, `#000000` (navy to black). The first build followed this, with blue and cyan accents (Observed). |
| Owner revision | **Black and white only**, running from white to black. No separate light and dark switch. No navy, no colored accent. Sections alternate between white and black bands. |
| Latest correction (**Pending** in the build) | Flat solid black areas look empty. Keep the black and white base, but give every section a gradient and add rich gradient mesh, aurora glows and soft color blobs as **background decoration only** (never text or button fills). |

### 6.2 Color tokens (target)

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `black` | `#000000` | Black sections, hero, primary buttons on white |
| `ink` | `#0A0A0A` | Body text on white, alternate black section |
| `charcoal` | `#171717` | Cards on black sections |
| `graphite` | `#262626` | Borders and hover states on black |
| `slate` | `#737373` | Muted text on white |
| `silver` | `#A3A3A3` | Muted text on black |
| `mist` | `#E5E5E5` | Borders and dividers on white |
| `snow` | `#F5F5F5` | Alternate white section, cards on white |
| `white` | `#FFFFFF` | White sections, primary buttons on black |

**Allowed background glow colors (decoration only, low to medium opacity, blurred, slowly drifting):** deep blue `#001242`, indigo `#4F46E5`, violet `#7C3AED`, cyan `#38BDF8`.

**Rules**

- Never use a flat solid black or flat solid white block as a large background. Use gradients (white to light gray, black to charcoal) and smooth gradient blends between sections, not hard edges.
- Add a fine film-grain texture and a faint grid or dot pattern on some black sections.
- Gradient borders and a soft colored glow on card hover.
- Text, icons and buttons stay black or white only.
- Keep contrast at WCAG AA or better.

### 6.3 Typography

| Use | Target font | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Headings | **Manrope** (weights 600 to 800) | Replaces the heading font in the first build, which the owner dislikes |
| Body and interface | **Inter** | Highly readable on phones |
| Small labels and numbers | **JetBrains Mono** | Used sparingly (the section labels in the footer and menu already use a monospace style) |

Do **not** use Poppins or Montserrat. Fall back to system fonts. Fonts are free from Google Fonts and should be self-hosted through the build where possible. Use a fluid size scale, tight line height on headings, and sentence case.

### 6.4 Components

- **Buttons:** primary is solid white on black bands and solid black on white bands, inverting smoothly on hover, with a magnetic pull toward the cursor. Secondary is an outline. Text links get an animated arrow.
- **Cards** (`Card.tsx`, one component with variants): service, case study, review, team, stat, next-step. Rounded corners (16 to 24 px), subtle glass or gradient surface, one-pixel border, consistent padding, hover and focus states on every card.
- **Icons:** one line-icon set (Lucide), black or white only.
- **Layout:** 8-pixel grid, generous space, maximum content width about 1200 px, one idea per section. Design at 360 px first, then scale up. Touch targets at least 44 px.

### 6.5 Animation

Built with Framer Motion and CSS. Required effects: page transitions, hero mesh and word-by-word headline, staggered scroll reveals (never over 600 ms), the infinite marquee, spotlight and tilt on cards, animated counters, the menu overlay's staggered reveal, the command palette's scale-in with a blurred backdrop, a scroll progress bar (`ScrollProgress.tsx`), link and arrow micro-interactions, and skeleton loaders for anything loaded from the database.

Rules: animate only `transform` and `opacity`, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, keep the page smooth on a mid-range phone, lazy-load heavy effects, and do not animate everything at once.

## 7. System architecture

### 7.1 Technology stack

| Layer | Choice | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Front end | React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS | Pages, components, styling |
| Animation | Framer Motion | Transitions, reveals, hover effects |
| Routing | React Router | Multi-page navigation, lazy-loaded routes |
| Database, login, files | Supabase (PostgreSQL, Auth, Storage) | Leads, reviews, portal data, uploads |
| Email notification | One serverless function in `api/` on Vercel | Emails the owner on new leads or reviews |
| Booking | Calendly embed | Book a call |
| Hosting | Vercel | Deploys from GitHub, free subdomain |
| Search | Fuse.js over a content index | Instant client-side search |
| Video | YouTube embeds with click-to-load cover | No video hosting cost |

### 7.2 How React and the "backend" fit together

React runs **only in the visitor's browser**. There is no custom server to maintain. Supabase is a backend-as-a-service: it provides the database, user accounts, file storage and the security rules that decide who can read or write what. One small serverless function (`api/notify.ts`) sends email. This keeps the project simple, free and easy to move.

```text
Visitor's browser (React app, hosted on Vercel)
        │
        ├── reads site content ──────────► src/content/*.ts  (bundled with the app)
        │
        ├── forms, reviews, portal ─────► src/services/database.ts ─► Supabase
        │                                  src/services/auth.ts     ─► Supabase Auth
        │                                  src/services/storage.ts  ─► Supabase Storage
        │
        ├── after a form is saved ──────► src/services/notify.ts ─► /api/notify (Vercel function) ─► email
        │
        └── booking ────────────────────► src/services/booking.ts ─► Calendly
```

### 7.3 Architecture principles

- **Content layer.** Pages read content through typed functions such as `getServices()`, never raw data. Today these read `src/content`; later they can read Supabase without touching any page.
- **Service layer.** `src/services/*` is the **only** code that talks to outside systems. `database.ts` defines an interface (create lead, list projects, submit review and so on) and Supabase is one implementation. Changing database, email or booking tool means rewriting one file.
- **Feature folders.** Each feature owns its pages, components and hooks.
- **Types first.** Shared TypeScript types in `src/types/index.ts` mirror the database tables.
- **Config over code.** Brand, links, emails, Calendly URL and feature switches live in `src/config/site.config.ts`.
- **Lazy routes** and code splitting for a small first load.
- **Demo mode.** If environment variables are empty, the app still runs: forms show success and log data, the portal uses sample data, and a banner (`DemoModeBanner.tsx`) says demo mode is on. The site also switches to live data as soon as keys are added.
- **Feature flags** such as `enablePortal`, `enableReviewsSubmission`, `enableBlog` and the extended-network switch.

## 10. Database design

Supabase (PostgreSQL). The database holds only data that changes while the site runs. The full SQL lives in `supabase/schema.sql` and `supabase/policies.sql`.

| Table | Key columns | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `profiles` | id (linked to the auth user), full_name, company, role (`client`, `team`, `freelancer`, `admin`), avatar_url, created_at | One row per signed-in user, created by a trigger on sign-up with role `client` |
| `leads` | id, name, email, company, service_slug, budget_range, message, attachment_path, source_page, status (`new`, `contacted`, `qualified`, `won`, `lost`), created_at | Quote requests |
| `reviews` | id, author_name, company, role_title, service_slug, rating (1 to 5), body, photo_path, status (`pending`, `approved`, `rejected`), featured, created_at, approved_at | Client reviews with approval |
| `projects` | id, client_id, title, service_slug, summary, status (`proposal`, `active`, `review`, `delivered`, `closed`), start_date, due_date, created_at | Client projects |
| `project_members` | project_id, user_id, member_role | Team members and freelancers on a project |
| `milestones` | id, project_id, title, description, status, due_date, sort_order | Progress steps |
| `deliverables` | id, project_id, milestone_id, title, file_path, published, created_at | Files clients download |
| `messages` | id, project_id, sender_id, body, created_at | Project conversation |
| `newsletter_subscribers` | id, email (unique), created_at | Footer signup |

### Relationships

```text
auth.users 1─1 profiles
profiles   1─* projects          (client_id)
projects   1─* milestones
projects   1─* deliverables      (optionally linked to a milestone)
projects   1─* messages
projects   *─* profiles          (through project_members)
```

### Required design qualities

- UUID primary keys; `created_at` and `updated_at` with an update trigger; foreign keys with sensible delete rules; check constraints on ratings and statuses; indexes on foreign keys, `status` and `created_at`.
- **Row-level security (RLS) on every table:**
  - Visitors can **insert** leads, pending reviews and newsletter signups, and can **read approved reviews only**.
  - A client reads only their own projects, milestones, published deliverables and messages.
  - Team members read projects they belong to.
  - Only admins read leads and manage everything, through a helper function `is_admin()`.
- **Storage buckets:** `attachments` (private, lead uploads), `deliverables` (private, signed download links), `review-photos` (public), `avatars` (public). Limit file size and type.
- `seed.sql` holds sample rows for demos only.
- The first admin is created by running a one-line SQL statement that sets the owner's profile role to `admin` (steps are in `OWNER_GUIDE.md`).

## 11. Integrations

### 11.1 Forms and email

1. Every form (quote, review, newsletter, contact) **saves to the database first**, so nothing is lost if email fails.
2. After saving, the front end calls `api/notify.ts`, which emails the owner through an email provider (Resend free tier was specified).
3. If email variables are empty, email is skipped silently and the lead is still visible in the admin area.
4. Spam protection: hidden honeypot field, minimum fill time, basic rate limit in the function, optional Cloudflare Turnstile behind a flag.

### 11.2 Booking

Calendly inline embed on `/contact` and in a modal from Book a Call buttons. The link is the single value `calendlyUrl` in `site.config.ts`. If empty, a fallback card shows the owner's email. Calendly has a free plan with limits; confirm what it includes today. Booking code is isolated in `src/services/booking.ts` so Cal.com or another tool can replace it.

### 11.3 Video and media

Maximum **three videos** on the whole site, defined in `content/videos.ts`. Each uses a click-to-load cover, then a privacy-friendly YouTube embed (`VideoEmbed.tsx`). No autoplay with sound. No self-hosted video files.

### 11.4 Search

Client-side index built from services, case studies, team and FAQs using Fuse.js. Shown in the command palette with grouped results, keyboard navigation and recent searches. No server calls.

## 12. Configuration and environment variables

Secrets live only in environment variables. Never commit real values. `.env.example` is the authority for names; the table below matches the eight variables shown in the project's Secrets panel.

### 12.1 Variable reference

| Variable | Runs in | Required | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` | Browser | Yes for live mode | Web address of your Supabase project |
| `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` | Browser | Yes for live mode | Supabase public (anon) key, restricted by row-level security |
| `VITE_SITE_URL` | Browser | Yes | The public address of the site (used for sign-in redirects and links) |
| `VITE_CALENDLY_URL` | Browser | Recommended | Your Calendly booking link |
| `OWNER_EMAIL` | Server (`api/notify.ts`) | Recommended | Inbox that receives lead and review notifications |
| `RESEND_API_KEY` | Server only | Recommended | Secret key for sending email through Resend |
| `FROM_EMAIL` | Server (`api/notify.ts`) | Recommended | The sender address on notification emails |
| `VITE_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` | Browser | Optional | Public key for Cloudflare Turnstile spam protection |

**Leave a variable empty and the matching feature switches off or falls back to demo mode.** Nothing breaks.

**Important:** every variable starting with `VITE_` is bundled into the browser code and visible to anyone. That is fine for the five above because they are public by design. Never put a secret in a `VITE_` variable. `OWNER_EMAIL`, `RESEND_API_KEY` and `FROM_EMAIL` have no `VITE_` prefix on purpose, so they stay on the server.

> **Check this:** Turnstile only blocks spam if the server also verifies the token. That needs a **secret key** (commonly named `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY`) read inside `api/notify.ts`. It is not in the list of eight variables. Open `api/notify.ts`: if it does not verify the token, either add that check and variable, or rely on the honeypot field, fill-time check and rate limit only. Do not assume Turnstile is protecting the forms until the server check exists.

### 12.2 Where to enter the values

| Where you are running the site | Where to enter variables |
|---|---|
| AI Studio preview | The **Secrets** panel (the Name and Value screen). Values are hidden once saved. Click **Apply** after adding or changing any value. |
| Your computer | Copy `.env.example` to `.env.local` and fill in the values. Restart `npm run dev` after every change. `.env.local` must stay listed in `.gitignore`. |
| Vercel (production) | Project, **Settings**, **Environment Variables**. Add each variable for Production, Preview and Development, then **redeploy** (changes do not apply to old deployments). |

Notes:

- The email function (`api/notify.ts`) is a Vercel serverless function. It will most likely not run in the AI Studio preview or under plain `npm run dev`, so test email on a Vercel deployment (or with the Vercel command line tool locally).
- Variable names must match exactly, including capitals and underscores.

### 12.3 How to obtain each value

#### Supabase: `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY`

1. Go to **supabase.com**, create a free account and click **New project**.
2. Choose an organization, a project name (for example `idata`), a strong database password (save it in a password manager) and the region nearest your clients.
3. Wait for the project to finish setting up.
4. Open **Project Settings**, then **API** (in newer dashboards this may be called **API Keys**).
5. Copy the **Project URL**. This is `VITE_SUPABASE_URL`. It looks like `https://abcdefgh.supabase.co`.
6. Copy the **anon public** key (newer dashboards may label it the **publishable** key). This is `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY`.
7. **Never** use the `service_role` or `secret` key in this project. It bypasses all security rules.
8. Build the database: open **SQL Editor** and run `supabase/schema.sql`, then `supabase/policies.sql`, then optionally `supabase/seed.sql` (sample data only).
9. Open **Storage** and create the buckets `attachments` (private), `deliverables` (private), `review-photos` (public) and `avatars` (public), unless the SQL already creates them.
10. Open **Authentication**, then **URL Configuration**. Set **Site URL** to your `VITE_SITE_URL` value and add every address you use (local, Vercel, later the .com) to **Redirect URLs**.
11. Optional Google sign-in: **Authentication**, **Providers**, **Google**. This needs a Google Cloud OAuth client; follow Supabase's on-screen steps.
12. Make yourself admin: sign up once on the site, then in the SQL Editor run the admin statement from `OWNER_GUIDE.md` (it sets your profile role to `admin`).

#### Site address: `VITE_SITE_URL`

This is simply the public web address of the site, with no trailing slash.

| Stage | Value |
|---|---|
| Local development | The address printed in your terminal, usually `http://localhost:5173` or `http://localhost:3000` |
| First deployment | Your Vercel address, for example `https://idata.vercel.app` |
| After you buy the .com | `https://yourdomain.com` |

Whenever this changes, update it in Vercel **and** in Supabase **URL Configuration**, then redeploy.

#### Calendly: `VITE_CALENDLY_URL`

1. Create a free account at **calendly.com**.
2. Connect your calendar (Google or Outlook), set your availability and your time zone.
3. Create an **event type**, for example a 30-minute call (the site's button says Book a 30-min Call).
4. Copy the event link, for example `https://calendly.com/yourname/30min`. That is the value.
5. Test it by opening the link in a private browser window.

The free plan has limits (for example on the number of event types). Confirm the current limits on Calendly's pricing page. If the value is empty, the site shows a fallback card with your email instead.

#### Resend (email): `RESEND_API_KEY`, `FROM_EMAIL` and `OWNER_EMAIL`

1. Create a free account at **resend.com** using the email address that should receive notifications.
2. Open **API Keys**, click **Create API Key**, choose **Sending access**, and name it (for example `idata-site`).
3. Copy the key **immediately**. It is shown only once and starts with `re_`. This is `RESEND_API_KEY`. If you lose it, create a new one.
4. **Starting out (no domain yet):** set `FROM_EMAIL` to Resend's test sender `onboarding@resend.dev` and set `OWNER_EMAIL` to the same address you used to sign up for Resend. In this test mode Resend only delivers to your own account email.
5. **Going professional (later):** in Resend, open **Domains**, add your domain, and add the DNS records it shows at your domain registrar. When it shows as verified, set `FROM_EMAIL` to something like `IData <notifications@yourdomain.com>`. After that, `OWNER_EMAIL` can be any inbox you check.
6. `OWNER_EMAIL` is simply the inbox where lead and review alerts arrive. Use an address you read daily and that is not the same as one that filters these messages to spam.

Resend's rules and free limits change, so confirm them in Resend's documentation.

#### Cloudflare Turnstile: `VITE_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` (optional)

1. Create a free account at **cloudflare.com** and open **Turnstile** from the dashboard menu.
2. Click **Add widget**. Name it `idata`. Under hostnames add `localhost`, your Vercel address and, later, your .com domain. Choose the **Managed** mode.
3. Cloudflare gives you two keys: a **Site Key** (public) and a **Secret Key** (private).
4. Put the **Site Key** in `VITE_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY`.
5. Put the **Secret Key** only on the server (see the check in 12.1), never in a `VITE_` variable.
6. For local testing, Cloudflare publishes dummy test keys in its Turnstile documentation, so you can test without real hostnames.
7. To switch Turnstile off, leave the variable empty.

### 12.4 Recommended order of setup

1. Run the site locally in demo mode (no variables).
2. Create the Supabase project and fill the two Supabase variables. Run the SQL and create the buckets.
3. Set `VITE_SITE_URL` and the Supabase URL Configuration.
4. Create the Calendly event and set `VITE_CALENDLY_URL`.
5. Create the Resend account and set the three email variables.
6. Optionally set up Turnstile.
7. Deploy to Vercel, enter every variable there, redeploy, then add the Vercel address to Supabase redirect URLs and to `VITE_SITE_URL`.
8. Run the verification checklist below.

### 12.5 Verification checklist

| Test | Expected result |
|---|---|
| Open the site after adding Supabase keys | The demo mode banner disappears |
| Submit a test quote | A new row appears in the `leads` table in Supabase |
| Same submission with email set up | An email arrives at `OWNER_EMAIL` (on Vercel) |
| Click Book a Call | The Calendly window opens with your event |
| Sign up and sign in on `/portal` | You arrive back on the live site after signing in |
| Submit a review | It appears as `pending` and is not public until approved |
| Try to read leads while signed out | Access denied (row-level security working) |
| Turnstile enabled | The check appears on the form, and the server rejects forms without a valid token |

### 12.6 Security rules for keys

- Never paste secret keys into chat, email, screenshots, public repositories or any `VITE_` variable.
- `.env.local` and `.env` must stay in `.gitignore`. Only `.env.example` (with empty values) is committed.
- If a key is ever exposed, revoke it in the provider's dashboard right away, create a new one, and update every place it is used.
- Only the Supabase **anon** key belongs in the browser.
- Non-secret settings (brand, links, feature flags) live in `src/config/site.config.ts`.

## 15. Free-tier limits and migration path

The first phase is **free everywhere**: free hosting, free database, free subdomain. Free tiers have limits, so the site is built to be efficient.

| Watch | Why |
|---|---|
| Database size and file storage | Free quotas are small; do not store large files |
| Bandwidth | Compress images, keep video on YouTube |
| Email volume | Free email plans cap monthly sends |
| Inactive projects | Free database projects may pause after a period of inactivity; check the provider's current policy |
| Calendly | The free plan has feature limits; confirm current terms |

Provider limits change, so always check each provider's current terms before relying on a number.

**Migration path (designed in from day one):**

- **Database or auth:** write a new implementation of the interface in `src/services/database.ts` and `auth.ts`, move the SQL, update environment variables. Pages do not change.
- **Email:** replace the provider call inside `api/notify.ts`.
- **Booking:** replace `src/services/booking.ts` (Cal.com or another tool).
- **Hosting:** the app is a standard Vite build and can move to any static host that supports serverless functions.
- **Content to CMS or database:** change the content functions to read from Supabase or a CMS. Pages are unaffected.

## 19. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Marquee** | The continuously scrolling strip of logos or tools (also called a logo ticker or logo slider) |
| **CTA** | Call to action: a button or card that pushes the visitor to the next step |
| **BaaS** | Backend as a service. Supabase provides the database, login and storage without a custom server |
| **RLS** | Row-level security. Database rules that decide which rows each user may read or write |
| **Demo mode** | The app running with no keys, using sample data |
| **Serverless function** | A small piece of server code that runs only when called (`api/notify.ts`) |
| **Anon key** | Supabase's public key, safe for the browser because RLS restricts what it can do |
| **Command palette** | The Ctrl+K or Cmd+K search overlay |
| **Feature flag** | A config switch that turns a feature on or off |
| **Design tokens** | Named colors, fonts and spacing values defined once and reused everywhere |
